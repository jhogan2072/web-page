Post-Dislocation Recovery · Shoulder Program

Shoulder Strength & Mobility Program

Combines home exercises from Professional Physical Therapy (Lauren Kelley, PT) with a self-directed strength progression. Organized by intensity level — move through Level 1, 2, and 3 as tolerated, not by calendar week.

Cleared for full activity Nerve recovery ongoing Full gym access

Ground Rules

  • Pain during exercise: stay at or below 3/10. Sharp pain = stop immediately.
  • Pain after exercise: should not linger more than 60 minutes post-session.
  • Clicks/catches: OK to work through if they're not sharp or worsening.
  • Deltoid fatigues fast — take full 90–120 sec rest between sets, no exceptions.
  • Use higher reps (15–20) and lighter weight where a range is given. Endurance before strength.
  • Progress only when you can complete all sets with pain ≤ 2/10.

How the Levels Work

Levels represent intensity and technical demand, not weeks. Start with Level 1 and make sure those exercises feel controlled and pain-free before adding Level 2 work, then Level 3.

Many Level 1 exercises — the rotation work and scapular exercises especially — are worth keeping in your rotation as a warm-up even once you've moved on to Level 2 and 3. Aim for 3–4 sessions per week with at least one rest day between sessions that hit similar movement patterns.

Level 1 — Foundational
Restore mechanics: gentle assisted range of motion, rotator cuff activation, scapular control. Sets/reps vary by exercise — see each card. Most of these can be done most days of the week.
01

Active Assisted Flexion

3 × 15 New
Dual-arm functional trainer · highest pulley position

Setup

Stand facing a dual-arm functional trainer with the pulley set to its highest position and a light weight selected on the stack. Attach a single handle and hold it with your involved hand, starting with your arm pulled down at your side.

Movement

Relax your arm and let the weight stack's own descent retract the cable back toward the pulley, lifting your arm up into flexion in front of you. Then actively pull the handle back down to your side against the weight, controlling the return.

Form Check

Keep your torso upright throughout — don't lean back or use your body to help the lift; let the weight stack do the work. Control the downward pull smoothly rather than letting the weight yank your arm down.

Tip Select just enough weight to comfortably lift your arm — too little and there's no real assist, too much and you'll fight the stack on the way down. As strength returns, reduce the weight, and eventually graduate to the Cable Front Raise in Level 2 for the unassisted version of this same movement.

02

Active Assisted Abduction

3 × 15 New
Dual-arm functional trainer · highest pulley position

Setup

Stand sideways next to a dual-arm functional trainer, involved arm closest to the machine, pulley set to its highest position with a light weight selected on the stack. Hold the handle with your involved hand, starting with your arm pulled down at your side.

Movement

Relax your arm and let the weight stack's own descent retract the cable back toward the pulley, guiding your arm up and out to the side into abduction. Then actively pull the handle back down to your side against the weight, controlling the return.

Form Check

Avoid shrugging your shoulder up toward your ear as the arm rises — let the weight stack do the lifting rather than compensating with your neck and traps.

Tip Same weight-selection principle as Active Assisted Flexion: enough to lift comfortably, not so much that lowering it back down feels like a fight.

03

Shoulder External Rotation

3 × 10 New
Dual-arm functional trainer · towel roll under arm

Setup

Stand sideways next to a dual-arm functional trainer with the pulley at elbow height, anchored on the side closest to your involved arm so the cable crosses in front of your body. Hold the handle with your elbow bent 90° and tucked at your side, a small towel roll wedged under your arm.

Movement

Rotate your forearm outward, away from the machine, pulling against the resistance. Hold briefly, then slowly return to the starting position.

Form Check

Keep hips and shoulders facing forward — don't let your torso rotate to help. Maintain a gentle chin tuck and avoid shrugging.

Tip This is one of the most important exercises for shoulder stability — it isolates the small rotator cuff muscles that keep the ball of the joint centered.

05

Standing Shoulder Internal Rotation Stretch with Towel

3 × hold 30 sec New
Towel · static stretch

Setup

Stand upright, holding both ends of a towel — one arm reaches up and behind your head, the other reaches behind your mid-to-low back.

Movement

Slowly straighten your top arm, gently pulling the towel upward with your bottom hand until you feel a stretch. Hold, then release.

Form Check

Keep your back straight throughout — avoid arching or leaning to manufacture extra range.

Movement

Slowly straighten your bottom arm, gently pulling downward on the towel until you feel a stretch through the top arm. Hold this position.

Prone Shoulder Extension on Swiss Ball with Dumbbells

3 × 10 New
Swiss ball · light dumbbells

Setup

Lie face-down with your chest resting on a swiss ball, feet on the floor behind you, holding a light dumbbell in each hand with arms hanging down.

Movement

Raise both arms straight backward as far as comfortable, then lower them back down and repeat.

Form Check

Keep your back straight and think about squeezing your shoulder blades together as your arms rise.

10

Scaption (Diagonal Raises)

3 × 12
Light dumbbell (1–3 lb)

Setup

Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, holding a light dumbbell in your involved hand, thumb turned slightly up.

Movement

Raise your arm along a diagonal plane about 30° in front of straight-out-to-the-side, up to shoulder height. Lower slowly over 3 seconds.

Form Check

Don't force range beyond shoulder height early on, and don't let the shoulder shrug toward the ear.

Tip Your deltoid may fatigue after 6–8 reps given the nerve recovery still in progress. If quality drops, stop the set early rather than grinding through — poor mechanics here can reinforce the impingement pattern you're working to fix.

Level 2 — Build Strength
Add load to the stabilizers, begin compound pulling patterns, and progress rotator cuff work into more functional positions. Introduce 1–2 of these at a time once Level 1 feels controlled and pain-free.
01

Face Pulls

3 × 20
Dual-arm functional trainer · rope or dual handles

Setup

Attach a rope to the functional trainer at roughly face height. Grasp both ends and step back until there's tension, arms extended in front of you.

Movement

Pull toward your face, leading with your elbows flaring out wide and up until your hands are near your ears. Hold briefly, then return slowly over 3 seconds.

Form Check

Elbows should finish at or above shoulder height at the end of the pull — that's what maximizes external rotation and posterior deltoid engagement.

Tip One of the best all-around exercises for your situation — it trains the posterior deltoid and external rotators together while working around the axillary nerve limitation.

02

Shoulder External Rotation at 90°

3 × 15
Dual-arm functional trainer

Setup

Set the pulley to elbow height. Stand sideways to the functional trainer, involved arm facing away from the machine, elbow raised to shoulder height and bent 90°.

Movement

Keeping the elbow fixed at shoulder height, rotate your forearm backward (externally) as far as comfortable. Return slowly.

Form Check

The elbow shouldn't travel during the movement — only the forearm rotates.

Tip This progresses the Level 1 external rotation into a more functional, elevated arm position — closer to where the shoulder actually works day to day.

03

Landmine Press

3 × 12
Barbell · landmine attachment

Setup

Anchor one end of a barbell in a landmine attachment or corner. Grip the other end with your involved hand and get into a half-kneeling position, knee on the same side as your pressing arm down.

Movement

Starting with the bar at shoulder height and elbow tucked, press at a 45° upward angle until your arm is almost fully extended, following the bar's natural arc. Lower slowly and under control.

Form Check

Don't let the bar drop on the way down — control the full range in both directions.

Tip Start with just the bar sleeve, no plates. The angle alone provides real resistance. Add a small plate only once the bar alone feels easy for all 3 sets.

04

Cable Front Raise

3 × 15
Dual-arm functional trainer · low pulley

Setup

Attach a single handle to a low pulley on the functional trainer. Stand facing away from the machine, gripping the handle with your involved arm, palm facing down or inward.

Movement

With a slight elbow bend, raise your arm forward to shoulder height. Hold briefly, then lower slowly over 3 seconds against the cable tension.

Form Check

Don't raise above shoulder height initially, and avoid leaning back to generate momentum.

Tip Front raises load the deltoid directly. Stop the set if form starts to break down before finishing all reps — don't push through fatigue here.

05

Seated Row

3 × 10–15 New
Dual-arm functional trainer · seated

Setup

Adjust the functional trainer's pulleys to a low, seated-row position. Sit upright with your back straight and knees slightly bent, gripping a handle in each hand with neutral wrists.

Movement

Pull the handles in toward your lower ribs, keeping your elbows close to your sides and squeezing your shoulder blades together in your mid-back.

Form Check

Keep your core engaged and your back in the same position throughout — don't lean back to help the pull. Choose a handle attachment that keeps your wrists comfortable.

06

Lat Pull Down

3 × 10–12 New
Dual-arm functional trainer · seated, high pulley

Setup

Set both pulleys of the functional trainer to their highest position and attach a bar. Sit facing the machine, grab the bar overhead with elbows straight, and secure your thighs under a pad or stable surface if available.

Movement

Pull your arms down against the resistance to shoulder height, bringing the bar in front of your body. Return slowly to the start.

Form Check

Keep your back straight and core engaged, pulling your shoulders down toward your hips rather than shrugging up. You should feel your back and outer rib cage working.

07

Triceps Extension

3 × 10–12 New
Dual-arm functional trainer · standing, high pulley

Setup

Set the functional trainer's pulley to a high position and attach a bar or rope. Stand facing the machine, grip the attachment, and bend your elbows to 90° with elbows tucked at your sides.

Movement

Press the attachment straight down toward the ground, then slowly return to the start position.

Form Check

Keep your back straight and chin gently tucked. Don't let your elbows drift forward away from your body.

Level 3 — Functional Strength
Build real-world shoulder strength and overhead capacity. Progress weight gradually and keep the ground rules — especially the pain and rest guidelines — front of mind at this stage.
01

Half-Kneeling Single Arm Press

3 × 10
Dumbbell or cable

Setup

Kneel on the knee of your involved side, holding a dumbbell in your involved hand at shoulder height.

Movement

Brace your core and press the dumbbell straight overhead, then lower slowly. The half-kneeling position prevents assisting with your lower back.

Form Check

Only press as high as you can without pain — work to eye level first, then progress toward full overhead as tolerated.

Tip It's completely normal not to be ready for full overhead pressing yet given where the axillary nerve recovery stands. Progress from eye level, to forehead level, to overhead — there's no need to rush this.
Watch video demo
02

Dumbbell Incline Press

3–4 × 12
Dumbbells · incline bench

Setup

Set a bench to a 30–45° incline. Lie back holding a dumbbell in each hand at shoulder level.

Movement

Press both dumbbells up and slightly inward so they come together near the top. Lower slowly, elbows at roughly 60° from your body.

Form Check

Stop the descent when your upper arms are parallel to the floor — don't force a deeper stretch than that.

Tip Track the weight difference between your two sides over time. As nerve recovery continues, that gap should gradually narrow — it's one of the best objective markers of progress you have.

03

Band Pull-Aparts

3 × 20
Light resistance band

Setup

Hold a resistance band in front of you with both hands, shoulder-width apart, arms extended at chest height.

Movement

Keeping arms straight, pull the band apart until it touches your chest, squeezing your shoulder blades together. Return slowly, maintaining tension throughout.

Form Check

Don't let the band snap back on the return — control it the whole way.

Progress Tracking Milestones

Signs You're Progressing Well

  • Less catching/clicking during raises
  • Increased pain-free range of motion
  • Stronger lateral raise on the involved side
  • Narrowing strength gap between sides on pressing movements
  • Deltoid no longer fatigues after just a few reps

Signs to Scale Back

  • Pain worsening after sessions
  • New clicking patterns
  • Increased aching at rest
  • Any pins and needles or increased numbness — contact your nerve doctor if neurological symptoms change

Important: This program is for general informational purposes and is based on your self-reported history, combined with exercises prescribed by Professional Physical Therapy. It does not replace medical advice. Contact your orthopedic surgeon or nerve specialist if you experience new neurological symptoms, sharp pain, or significant regression.
